# Theroux — product demo script

**Target: 4:00–4:30. Unlisted YouTube.**
Screen recording of `dashboard/theroux.html` with voiceover. No slides, no face,
no intro card. Open on the product.

**Before you hit record**

- `python3 run.py` so the dashboard is current
- Open `dashboard/theroux.html` in Chrome, **not** `index.html`
- Full screen, browser zoom 100%, hide bookmarks bar (⌘⇧B)
- Have the **Score** view's text box already cleared
- Practise once. The clicks are choreographed and fumbling reads as a broken product.

Timings are the outer edge. Shorter is better.

---

## 0:00 — 0:25 · What it is (Coverage view, top)

> "This is Theroux. It measures constrained disclosure — the linguistic residue left
> when someone is holding information they can't state directly.
>
> The core idea is that it scores every statement against a model of *that speaker's
> own language*, not against an absolute standard. The question is never 'is this
> evasive.' It's 'is this evasive **for this person**.'
>
> To be clear about what this is not: it does not detect lies. It never claims anyone
> was untruthful."

*Scroll slowly through the header stats. Do not click yet.*

---

## 0:25 — 0:55 · The data is real (Coverage view, queue)

> "This is running on 472 Federal Reserve speeches — every Board speech since 2021.
> Eleven speakers with fitted baselines. Powell, Bowman, Waller, Barr, Cook.
> 1.2 million words.
>
> We picked the Fed for one reason: **we don't decide who's constrained.** The FOMC
> publishes a blackout calendar in advance — no policy comment from the second
> Saturday before a meeting through the day after. Treatment assignment is arithmetic
> on a public calendar, not our judgement.
>
> And it holds format constant. These officials give the same kind of speech inside
> and outside blackout, which is a confound we could not fix any other way."

*Scroll the queue. Point at the signed divergence bars.*

> "Right of centre is more evasive than that speaker's own norm. Left is more direct
> than usual — which is its own signal, and most tools can't represent it."

---

## 0:55 — 1:35 · Why a flag fired (click top row → Speaker → click a constrained row → Reading)

*Click the top row. Land on Speaker.*

> "Every speaker has a fingerprint. Grey band is one standard deviation of their own
> baseline, grey ticks are their individual past speeches, the dots are constrained
> windows."

*Click a constrained row in the statement history. Land on Reading.*

> "This is the view that matters. Same speaker, two registers. On the left, a speech
> from her own baseline. On the right, one delivered inside an FOMC blackout.
>
> The highlighting is generated by the scorer, not chosen by me. Every mark is a
> phrase the engine counted. Every monospaced figure is a concrete marker it credited
> toward specificity."

*Pause two full seconds on the two panes. Let the viewer read.*

> "You don't need the statistics to see it. They're underneath if you want them —
> every number traces to a counted phrase or a fitted parameter."

## 1:35 — 2:30 · It's a tool, not a report (Score view)

*Click **Score**.*

> "This isn't a static report. The model ships inside the page, so it scores live —
> offline, nothing sent anywhere, no transcript leaves the machine."

*Click **Hedged**. Let it score.*

> "Paste any text, pick any speaker, and it's scored against that person's real
> longitudinal baseline in the browser."

*Scroll to **The same words, said by different people**. Slow down here.*

> "And this is the whole argument in one panel. Identical text, scored against all
> eleven baselines. The same sentences span several standard deviations — because a
> habitual hedger saying hedged things is unremarkable, and a speaker who normally
> quotes basis points saying the same words is the signal.
>
> An absolute scorer — which is what transcript-sentiment vendors ship — returns one
> number for all eleven and cannot tell them apart."

*Click **Concrete**, then **Flat refusal**, so the numbers visibly move.*

---

## 2:30 — 3:10 · What breaks it (Simulation view)

*Click **Simulation**.*

> "We also model the speaker as a strategic agent, because the first question anyone
> asks is what happens when they know you're measuring them.
>
> Under adoption, register collapses — speakers stop hedging and start saying 'I won't
> answer that.' But correlation with what's actually withheld barely moves. The
> signature migrates out of the lexicon rather than disappearing.
>
> That's two businesses with opposite decay curves. A trading customer sees a
> degrading signal. A compliance customer sees the product working — it pushes people
> from talking around a subject to declining it on the record."

*Scroll to the design grid.*

> "And it answers the question we actually needed answered: fourteen speakers with six
> baseline statements each gets you to 88% power. That's a procurement target, not an
> apology."

*Scroll to the false-positive panel if visible.*

> "The case that matters is the habitual hedger who's withholding nothing. At a
> matched alarm budget, scoring against a speaker's own norm cuts false positives
> against absolute scoring. That gap is the product argument as a number you can
> check."

---

## 3:10 — 4:00 · What we found, including the part that failed (Method view)

*Click **Method**. Slow down. This is the section that earns trust.*

> "Now the honest part.
>
> One clean positive finding, and it needs no linguistics at all. The blackout covers
> 28% of the calendar and contains 5% of the speeches. Fed officials speak nearly six
> times less while the constraint is in force. The constraint is real and binding,
> established from a published calendar and a count.
>
> The linguistic test did **not** work. Our model predicts divergence should rise as
> the meeting approaches. Measured within-speaker across five officials, it falls.
> Median rho minus 0.8. That's the opposite of what we predicted, and we're reporting
> it because a team that hides its own negative result can't be trusted with the
> positive ones.
>
> We can't yet separate two explanations. Either the hypothesis is wrong, or the
> officials who speak during blackout are self-selected — delivering the safest,
> most pre-cleared remarks they have. Heavier vetting closer to a meeting would
> *reduce* divergence from self, which predicts exactly the sign we see.
>
> That's testable. Press conferences and Q&A are extempore rather than staff-drafted,
> and that's the next corpus.
>
> Two of our four method-validation studies also fail. The estimator recovers a
> planted ranking at rho 0.51, so the machinery is correct. But the claim that
> per-speaker baselining beats absolute scoring is not demonstrated at this sample
> size, and we say so on the page rather than in a footnote."

## Rules for the recording

**Do not** say "AI-powered", "revolutionary", or "unprecedented". The reviewers
build this for a living and the language reads as noise.

**Do not** claim the method works. It is not significant, and the dashboard says so
in the footer. Claiming otherwise while your own page contradicts you is the single
fastest way to lose a technical reviewer.

**Do** pause after the Reading view and after the same-words panel. Those are the two
moments the idea lands, and both need a beat of silence.

**Do** let the negative result sit. Do not rush past it apologetically. Delivered
flatly it is the most credible thirty seconds in the video.

If you fluff a line, stop and restart that section. Do not fix it in the edit —
jump cuts in a four-minute demo read as something being hidden.

## Recording setup

QuickTime → File → New Screen Recording, or ⌘⇧5. Record the browser window only,
not the full desktop. Built-in mic is fine; sit close to it. Export at 1080p.

Upload to YouTube as **Unlisted**. Title: `Theroux — constrained disclosure
measurement (Palantir Startup Fellowship)`. Paste the GitHub link in the description.
